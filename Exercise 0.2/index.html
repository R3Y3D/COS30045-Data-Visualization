Understanding Household Energy Use in Australia

Australian households spend an average of $1,600 to $2,200 per year on electricity. Understanding the power draw (wattage) of standard appliances helps minimize peak tariff costs.

Australian Tariffs

Average residential electricity prices range between 28c and 38c per kWh depending on your state and whether you are on time-of-use rates.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Click on any question below to reveal the answer.

A kilowatt-hour (kWh) is the standard unit of energy electricity retailers use for billing. Running a 1,000 Watt (1 kW) appliance continuously for 1 hour consumes exactly 1 kWh of electricity.

Average electricity rates in Australia vary between 28c/kWh and 38c/kWh depending on your state (e.g., NSW, VIC, QLD, WA) and tariff arrangement (flat rate vs. peak/off-peak time-of-use).

Standby power (or "vampire draw") is electricity consumed by electronic devices while turned off or in standby mode. Switching appliances off at the wall switch can save Australian households up to $100 annually.

The Energy Rating Label shows a star score from 1 to 10 stars. The higher the number of stars, the more energy-efficient the model is compared to other appliances of similar size.
