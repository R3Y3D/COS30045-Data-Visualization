Comparative Storyboard: Size vs. Display Technology

Structured exactly using Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic's (2015) 6-stage storytelling framework.

Phase 1: Narrative Planning

Dual Visual Storyboards (Knaflic 2015 Framework)

Both analytical tracks strictly adhere to Knaflic's six stages: Issue, Demonstrate Issue, Ideas for Overcoming Issue, Describe Program, Show Before/After Survey, and Recommendation.

Track A: Screen Size vs. Energy (Question 2)
1. Issue
Consumers upgrade to 65"+ living room TVs without realizing screen area scales electricity use non-linearly, causing surprise bill spikes.
2. Demonstrate Issue
KNIME bar chart (Figure 1): Large displays (~750 kWh/yr) consume roughly 6× more power than Small ones (~130 kWh) and double Medium ones.
3. Ideas for Overcoming Issue
Create an interactive web portal translating abstract kWh into annual Australian dollars ($32¢/kWh) and Star Rating comparisons.
4. Describe Program (Goals & Scope)
: Deploy the "EcoWatts TV Calculator" web tool in local retail stores to show 10-year running costs at point-of-sale.
5. Show Before & After Survey
Survey of 150 shoppers: Energy awareness rose from 22% before the pilot tool to 78% after using the interactive web app.
6. Recommendation
Retailers should mandate digital GEMS lifecycle cost displays beside price tags; shoppers choosing 65"+ must seek 7+ Star models.
Track B: Panel Technology vs. Energy (Question 3)
1. Issue
Shoppers believe marketing claims that premium display tech (OLED / Mini-LED) is automatically energy-efficient across all screen sizes.
2. Demonstrate Issue
KNIME pivot chart (Figure 2): In small screens, OLED draws more; in large screens, LCD (LED) draws the highest peak backlighting power.
3. Ideas for Overcoming Issue
Provide an educational side-by-side technology matrix breaking down self-emissive pixels vs. LED diffusers and ambient light sensors.
4. Describe Program (Goals & Scope)
: In-store interactive display comparison guide educating buyers on panel efficiency matched to room brightness levels.
5. Show Before & After Survey
Shopper confusion dropped: 81% mistook OLED for the most efficient large panel before; only 19% did after reviewing the guide.
6. Recommendation
Consumers should match panel choice to viewing conditions: choose standard LED for bright daylight rooms and OLED for controlled dark cinema rooms.

Phase 3: Synthesis & Narrative Takeaways

What The Combined Story Tells The Consumer

Screen size sets the total power baseline, while panel technology defines operating efficiency within that baseline.

Comparing both tracks yields clear takeaways for Australian buyers:

  • Physical Area is King: Doubling screen diagonal roughly quadruples surface area, meaning even an efficient large television consumes far more electricity than a small display.
  • Pilot Tools Work: As demonstrated in the survey data, giving consumers interactive running cost calculators transforms passive buyers into energy-conscious decision-makers.
  • Actionable Selection: Size the screen to the viewing room first, choose the panel technology based on room lighting, and confirm at least 7 GEMS stars for large formats.
